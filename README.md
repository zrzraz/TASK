T.A.S.K. is an in-development web application designed to help students discover events, clubs, learning opportunities, and community programs offered by local libraries and community organizations.

## Status: 🚧 MVP in development

# Problem 
<p> Many students struggle to balance school, extracurriculars, hobbies, and friendships, and at the same time, they’re losing access to safe and meaningful “third spaces” outside of home and school. </p>

# Solution 
<p> My solution is an app that connects public schools with their local libraries, creating a trusted platform where students can easily discover events, clubs, learning opportunities, and community projects happening in their neighborhood. Think of it like a mix of ClassDojo, Libby, and MyON—but focused on real-world experiences and community engagement. 
The value is that it gamifies learning outside the classroom, encourages civic involvement, and helps students build friendships and interests in a safe, supportive environment. By making local opportunities visible and accessible, we can help every student rediscover libraries as vibrant third spaces where they can learn, connect, and grow.
</p>

### What INSPIRED me? Tune in for my Personal Story ###

<p> ~kids don’t have third spaces anymore~ </p>

<p> This idea stems from my own experience when I first came back from Bangladesh in 5th grade. The number one system which eased my transition back into the US was my local library (Queens Library). My brothers were ESL students who learned English really fast thanks to the library initiatives, the librarians who would help them with their homework and then would allow unlimited game time, movie nights every single day. We also enjoyed perks such as Broadway shows. We got to see Matilda and Aladdin, something which we would've never gotten to experience coming from a low income household. </p>

<p> I'm forever grateful for my local library in making my 5th grade year so memorable, but everything changed right after I got to middle school. The librarians I grew such a close bond with, transferred to different branches or grew to different paths. Since then, I've only cherished the beautiful experience I had and haven't gotten to revisit this third space which I loved so much. </p>

<p> Queens Library and New York Public library websites do promote events and resources that they have. But, there isn’t really a trusted all-in-one platform where it's all accessible in one place, which is why combining schools with their local libraries is crucial. In addition to this, the events are rarely advertised such that it is accessible and known to everyone to benefit from. This is what's missing. Every neighborhood should benefit from the same perks and support which I received my 5th grade year and all kids should experience a wide variance of interests and hobbies to explore and learn more about themselves before they even enter College. </p>

<p> I think it would be so helpful to have an all-in-one platform which is trusted and loved for students of all ages to connect and find activities to do with like-minded people! </p>
